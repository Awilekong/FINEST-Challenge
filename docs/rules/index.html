Competition Rules

These rules define the task scope, permitted training resources, evaluation interface, scoring and submission requirements for the FINEST Challenge.

On this page1. Task scope2. Training policy3. Policy interface4. Scoring and ranking5. Hidden evaluation6. Submissions7. Fairness and verification8. Release configuration

01 Task Scope

The competition is conducted entirely in simulation. Episodes begin with the part securely grasped above its target and focus on alignment, contact and insertion. Researchers and student teams can participate without access to a physical robot.

02 Training Policy

Classical, learning-based and hybrid methods are welcome through the same observation and action interface. Imitation learning, reinforcement learning and self-generated simulation data are permitted. Teams may submit task-specific policies or a unified multi-task policy.

External datasets and pretrained models are permitted when their source, version, license and use are disclosed. Teams must also report training resources. Privileged simulator state may be used during training. Hidden evaluation information may not be used for training or model selection.

2.1 Data and asset licensing

Organizer-owned code will be released under Apache-2.0. Demonstration datasets and original assets will use CC BY 4.0. Third-party assets retain their original terms; the release manifest will specify attribution and redistribution conditions.

3.1 Evaluation-time access

At test time, policies may only access the prescribed observation interface. Ground-truth target poses, hidden configuration values, random seeds, simulator contact states, success flags and other evaluator internals are inaccessible to the policy.

4.1 Ranking and ties

Rank teams by the unrounded OverallScore. If scores are equal, compare the minimum weighted task score across the ten tasks; the higher minimum ranks first. Teams still tied share the same rank.

Per-task and per-clearance success rates are reported as diagnostics. The 0.01 mm extreme-precision results never enter ranking comparisons.

4.2 Worked example

A peg task with success rates of 0.90, 0.60 and 0.20 receives s(t) = 0.46 and contributes 4.6 points to the overall score.

The 20% / 30% / 50% weights are a FINEST competition design choice.

05 Hidden Evaluation

Final evaluation covers 20 task–clearance configurations with 100 episodes each, for 2,000 main-score episodes per team. All teams are evaluated on identical hidden instances using a frozen system. Independent final hidden evaluation determines the official ranking.

Initial poses and friction vary within published ranges. The final evaluation instances and random seeds remain private. Teams may generate training data across the published ranges; the hidden test measures robustness to held-out instances rather than guaranteeing that every test condition lies outside a team’s training distribution.

5.1 Episode outcomes

A successful episode must satisfy the task-specific completed-assembly condition within the episode limit. Missing tasks or episodes, dropped parts, invalid actions, policy errors and timeouts count as failures. Missing episodes remain in the denominator.

Evaluation infrastructure failures are rerun on the original instances using the same frozen system. Teams may not select only successful rollouts or combine the best results from different submissions.

06 Submissions

Each team may submit one system per development round and one frozen final system. Multiple task-specific checkpoints are allowed within that system.

6.1 Submission package

  • Inference code and model weights.
  • A container or complete environment specification.
  • A method report describing the approach.
  • External-data and pretrained-model declarations, including sources, versions and licenses.
  • Training and inference resource statements.

The evaluation record retains checkpoint hashes, software versions and run logs. Submissions must reproduce the declared inference behavior in the evaluation environment.

07 Fairness and Verification

All submissions run on common evaluation hardware with external networking disabled. The policy process is isolated from simulator and evaluator internals.

Leading systems are rerun for verification. Organizers audit observation access, inference artifacts, method descriptions and resource declarations. Prohibited access or unresolved reproducibility failures makes a submission ineligible.

Organizers, co-organizers and their direct advisees may not compete in this challenge. Rule updates and clarifications are shared publicly with all teams.

08 Release Configuration

The hardware profile, runtime limits, observation preprocessing, success tolerances, hold durations and perturbation ranges will be frozen and published before launch following a readiness test.

The frozen evaluation configuration will be published before launch.
